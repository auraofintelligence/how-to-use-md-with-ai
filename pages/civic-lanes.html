Choose what stays private and what helps the public.

A Markdown file can help an artificial intelligence tool understand what you mean, respect your privacy, and prepare clearer public records. The aim is simple: people keep control of their own story, and public power leaves a trail people can follow.

Choose a lane Build a starter file

This page is for everyday people.

You might use a phone, a notebook, a Facebook post, a printed notice, a community meeting, a wall screen or a conversation at the shops. You do not need to be technical. The point is to write down what is private, what can be shared, and what needs checking before an artificial intelligence tool helps with the words.

Local examples, open for the greater good

Many examples here come from North Stradbroke Island / Minjerribah because that is the community being designed for first. The pattern is open-source: people across Australia can copy it, adapt it and improve it for their own town, suburb, club, campaign, service, co-op or community group.

For people who find this from anywhere

You may arrive through word of mouth, social media, news, a community meeting or Purple Party for Australia. A clear public note should use full words, local context, source links, names of human reviewers and a simple way to correct mistakes.

Plain words first

These ideas become easier when the words are clear. A file is just a note. A public notice is just an approved message. A receipt is just proof people can check later.

Markdown file

A plain text note with headings. The file ending is usually `.md`. It can be opened, read, copied and edited without special software.

Artificial intelligence

A computer tool that can draft, sort, summarise or compare text. It works better when you give it the facts, boundaries and sources first.

Private support note

A private note about needs, preferences, care, reminders, goals and boundaries. In this wider ecosystem this can also be called an aura.

Digital record

A careful record of a person, place, project or community asset. It can begin as a folder of notes before it becomes anything more advanced.

Public profile or game character

The approved version of a person, project or group that appears on a website, noticeboard, app or game world.

Permission and consent

The person or group decides what can be shared, who can use it, who can change it, and how mistakes can be corrected.

A North Stradbroke Island example

A local group wants to organise a working bee, invite volunteers and share an update afterwards. The private file keeps organiser phone numbers, health needs and internal notes. The trusted file helps the small team prepare the day. The public notice tells locals what is happening, where to go and who has approved the message. The receipt records what was promised, what happened and what still needs follow-up.

That is the heart of the system. The artificial intelligence tool can help draft, shorten, translate, check sources and prepare versions for a wall screen or phone post. People keep the final say.

Needs, boundaries, helpers, risks and details that stay with the organiser.

From private support note to public accountability

The wider ecosystem can grow from ordinary files. A private support note helps a person stay in control. A public profile becomes an approved doorway. A noticeboard becomes shared memory. A game-world action can support useful civic life. A receipt trail becomes public accountability.
